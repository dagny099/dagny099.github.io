<h1>I help teams ship AI systems that humans actually trust.</h1>
<p class="section-intro">
Most AI initiatives fail not because of bad models, but because the knowledge underneath was never made legible — to the machines processing it, or to the humans acting on it. I close that gap, and I build systems that measure their own accuracy before anyone is asked to trust them.
</p>
</section>

    <p class="eyebrow">🛠️ What you can hire</p>
    <h2 class="section-title">Three ways to work together</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">Each is a shaped engagement with a clear deliverable, not a category. Many teams start with the audit or the workshop and move into a build once we both know what's there.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="content-slab">
    <p class="eyebrow">Offer 1</p>
    <h3>Knowledge Legibility Audit</h3>
    <p class="card-meta"><strong>For teams asking:</strong> "Are we actually ready to use AI on this knowledge?" · <strong>Shape:</strong> fixed-scope, typically 2–3 weeks</p>
    <p>Before you fund a build, find out what the AI would actually be reasoning over. I trace where your knowledge really lives — documents, spreadsheets, tickets, databases, and the parts that exist only in people's heads — and assess whether it's structured and traceable enough for a system to retrieve, cite, and get right.</p>
    <p><strong>You walk away with:</strong> a legibility map of your knowledge, a prioritized list of gaps (what's missing, ambiguous, or contradicts itself), and a concrete recommendation — build now, fix these things first, or don't build yet. Either way, you'll know why.</p>
    <p class="card-meta"><strong>Why it matters:</strong> most stalled AI projects didn't fail at the model. They failed a year earlier, when nobody checked whether the knowledge was legible. I wrote about that pattern in <a class="text-link" href="/blog/missing-layer-ai-adoption-value/">The Missing Layer</a>.</p>
    <p class="card-meta"><em>Start with the self-check: <a class="text-link" href="/resources/knowledge-legibility-diagnostic/">Is Your Knowledge Ready for AI?</a> · Related work: <a class="text-link" href="/blog/metadata-matters/">Metadata Matters</a> · <a class="text-link" href="/assets/resources/schema-org-cheatsheet-builders.pdf">Schema.org cheatsheet</a></em></p>
  </div>

  <div class="content-slab">
    <p class="eyebrow">Offer 2</p>
    <h3>Verified AI System Build</h3>
    <p class="card-meta"><strong>For teams that need:</strong> a RAG system, knowledge assistant, evaluation harness, or AI-enabled data product — one that earns trust before it goes live · <strong>Shape:</strong> fixed-scope, typically 6–12 weeks depending on scope; solo or alongside your team</p>
    <p>I build the system and its verification layer together, from day one — not as an afterthought. That means a golden question set with known-correct answers, an evaluation harness that scores the system against it, source citations on every response, and provenance you can trace. The AI doesn't go live until it passes.</p>
    <p><strong>You walk away with:</strong> a working system, its evaluation suite, documentation your team can actually use, and the knowledge transfer to own and extend all of it. No dependency on me after handoff — that's the point.</p>
    <p class="card-meta"><strong>Proof this is how I actually work:</strong> the <a class="text-link" href="/projects/poolula-platform/">Poolula platform</a> runs every query type through an evaluation harness before deployment; my <a class="text-link" href="https://twin.barbhs.com">digital twin</a> has <a class="text-link" href="/blog/twin-evaluate-models/">published model evaluations</a> and a documented <a class="text-link" href="/blog/twin-graphrag-migration/">GraphRAG migration</a>; the <a class="text-link" href="/projects/fitness-dashboard/">fitness dashboard</a> keeps every metric one click from how it was calculated.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="content-slab">
    <p class="eyebrow">Offer 3</p>
    <h3>AI Adoption &amp; Data Readiness Workshop</h3>
    <p class="card-meta"><strong>For teams that need:</strong> a shared model of what AI can and can't do with your knowledge, before committing budget to a build · <strong>Shape:</strong> half a day to two days, remote or on-site</p>
    <p>We work with your real documents and workflows, not generic slides. The workshop builds a common vocabulary across technical and non-technical people, maps your candidate AI use cases, and ranks them honestly by readiness — including the ones that aren't ready and what it would take to change that.</p>
    <p><strong>You walk away with:</strong> a shared language for talking about AI readiness, a ranked map of use cases with the gaps named, and a clear decision about the next step. Workshops often lead into an audit or a build — but "not yet, and here's why" is a legitimate outcome, and a cheap one to learn early.</p>
    <p class="card-meta"><em>Related thinking: <a class="text-link" href="/blog/ai-literacy-judgment/">AI literacy is mostly judgment</a> · <a class="text-link" href="/ai-pulse-q2-2026/">The quiet number in KPMG's AI Pulse</a></em></p>
  </div>
